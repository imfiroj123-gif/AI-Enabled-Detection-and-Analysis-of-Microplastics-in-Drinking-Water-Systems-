Scientific Background

Microplastics in Potable Water Infrastructure

Microplastics are synthetic polymer particles ranging from 1 micrometer (µm) to 5 millimeters (mm). Despite conventional municipal coagulation and filtration, microscopic fibers and fragments penetrate finished drinking water networks.

Fibers (Synthetic Textiles)

Elongated particles shed during synthetic laundry cycles (polyester, nylon, acrylic). Fibers represent over 60–80% of microplastics detected in tap water due to their high aspect ratio and buoyancy.

Fragments (Degraded Plastics)

Hard, irregular, sharp-edged particles formed by photo-oxidation, mechanical shear, and environmental weathering of larger plastic debris (PE, PP, PVC) entering raw reservoirs.

Films (Packaging & Membranes)

Ultra-thin, flexible planar sheets typically derived from degraded agricultural mulches, grocery bags, and plastic food wraps that bypass sedimentation basins.

Beads & Microspheres

Uniform spherical polymers historically manufactured for cosmetics, abrasive cleaners, industrial air-blasting, or medical carriers that accumulate in effluent pipelines.

Pipeline Architecture

Three-Stage Analytical Workflow

From vacuum membrane filtration to calibrated optical quantification, our digital pipeline provides rapid automated candidate screening.

01

1. Ingest Sample Micrograph

Capture membrane filter slides using calibrated brightfield or Nile Red fluorescence microscopy. Submit high-resolution imagery along with sample volume (L), date, and catchment location.

02

2. Vision Segmentation & Classification

Deep learning neural networks process the micrograph to separate particles from mineral background, extract bounding boxes, classify morphological class, and estimate major/minor axes.

03

3. Review Quantitative Reports

Examine high-resolution bounding box annotations, particle concentration (particles/L), size distribution histograms, and export printable laboratory certificates or CSV datasets.

Analytical Rigor

Why Optical AI Screening Precedes Spectroscopy

Gold-standard polymer characterization methods such as Micro-Raman and Micro-FTIR spectroscopy are chemically definitive, but manual scanning of a 47 mm membrane filter can take up to 14 hours per sample.

Our computer vision framework acts as an ultra-fast high-throughput pre-screener: identifying coordinates of candidate particles in under 45 seconds, enabling targeted laser micro-spectroscopy only where plastic candidate targets are localized.

⚠️ Scientific Limitations & Safety Threshold Notice: Optical micrograph analysis identifies candidate microplastic particles based on morphology, aspect ratio, and fluorescence reflectance. Optical detection alone cannot provide 100% polymer confirmation (such as differentiating natural cellulose fibers from synthetic polyethylene). Furthermore, global regulatory bodies (including the WHO) have not established legally enforceable human health thresholds for microplastics in tap water. Hence, this platform explicitly reports candidate counts and concentrations rather than arbitrary binary "safe/unsafe" designations.
